Commentary

Understanding cannabis prices across legal markets

Cannabis prices vary dramatically across U.S. legal markets, with flower ranging from $2.12 per gram in Michigan to $14.23 per gram in Minnesota. This price tracker compiles official data from state cannabis regulatory agencies to provide the most accurate, up-to-date pricing information available for legal cannabis markets. Michigan now sets the national floor — its highly competitive adult-use market has compressed prices below even Oklahoma's structurally oversupplied medical-only program.

Why cannabis prices differ by state

Cannabis price variation across states is primarily driven by federal prohibition of interstate commerce. Unlike most consumer goods, cannabis cannot be transported across state lines, forcing each state to operate as an isolated market with its own production capacity. This creates dramatic price disparities between efficient and inefficient state regulatory frameworks.

Licensing density and market structure are the dominant factors in pricing. States with restrictive licensing caps create artificial scarcity that elevates prices, while markets with competitive licensing see prices driven down through market forces. Market maturity compounds this effect - established markets like Colorado and Oregon have developed efficient cultivation operations and distribution networks over years, while newer markets like Maryland and Minnesota face higher prices as infrastructure develops.

Regional production costs play a secondary but significant role. West Coast states benefit from favorable outdoor growing climates and lower energy costs for year-round production. East Coast and Midwest markets face higher overhead expenses for indoor cultivation facilities, climate control, and energy. Some states implement wholesale excise taxes collected at the cultivation level, which are embedded in retail pricing, but these represent a minor component compared to structural market efficiency factors.

State-by-state cannabis price analysis

Low-price markets (under $5/gram)

Michigan ($2.12/gram) sets the national floor — a highly competitive adult-use market driven by a large number of licensed cultivators and minimal supply constraints. The figure is the state's adult-use average retail flower price ($59.99/oz) from the Cannabis Regulatory Agency's September 2026 Monthly Report, converted to a per-gram basis. Michigan's pricing has fallen below even Oklahoma's medical-only program, underscoring how mature, license-permissive adult-use markets can outpace structurally oversupplied medical systems on price.

Oklahoma ($2.42/gram) remains among the cheapest markets in the country — a medical-only program operating under extreme structural oversupply. With over 2,100 licensed growers and no cultivation caps, Oklahoma produced an estimated 884 million grams of packaged cannabis in 2023 against licensed patient demand of roughly 50 million grams, per a state-commissioned supply and demand study. Low barriers to entry ($2,500 grower license fee, no patient qualifying conditions list) drove a historic supply glut that has compressed prices to levels below any adult-use market in the country except Michigan. The price is calculated from September 2026 Metrc data via the OMMA Dashboard: Flower & Bud retail sales divided by retail flower volume ($19.46M ÷ 8.04M g).

Colorado ($3.14/gram) represents a mature Western market with established supply chains and efficient production.

Oregon ($3.29/gram) benefits from favorable growing conditions and competitive licensing.

Massachusetts ($3.81/gram) maintains competitive pricing despite its East Coast location through a well-developed cultivation infrastructure and active retail competition.

New Mexico ($4.04/gram) stands as one of the most competitive adult-use markets in the nation — a weighted average compiled from over 85 million BioTrack retail records by the Cannabis Policy & Practice Center (CPPC) on behalf of the NM Cannabis Control Division shows the price collapsed 61.92% from $10.61 per gram at market open in April 2022, driven by permissive cultivation licensing, favorable outdoor growing conditions, and a highly competitive retail environment. The figure excludes sales and excise tax.

Mid-range markets ($5–$8/gram)

Nevada ($5.11/gram) reflects pricing in a mature Western market anchored by Las Vegas tourism, where high foot traffic and a well-established dispensary network have kept prices competitive; per the UNLV 2024 Cannabis Economy Overview, the state generated over $1 billion in annual retail sales.

Montana ($5.34/gram) reflects pricing in a maturing Western market with tourism-driven demand.

Illinois ($5.47/gram) shows pricing typical of a maturing Midwest market where cultivation costs are elevated.

Rhode Island ($5.99/gram) shows pricing for a small Northeast market that launched adult-use sales in December 2022.

Maine ($6.07/gram) reflects pricing dynamics in a smaller East Coast market with limited economies of scale.

California ($6.19/gram) reflects pricing in the nation's largest cannabis market, where a 15% state excise tax, local taxes that can exceed 10% in some municipalities, and high regulatory compliance costs push retail flower prices well above what the state's massive cultivation capacity would otherwise produce. The price is derived from packaged eighths* ($21.66 ÷ 3.5 g) — the most common consumer format by volume. Bulk half-ounce pricing reaches a floor of $3.14/g but represents approximately 20% of units sold.

Mississippi ($6.43/gram) is a medical-only program whose price is derived from the MMCP CY 2025 Annual Report average unit cost for Bud/Flower/Shake/Trim ($22.50 ÷ 3.5g standard eighth-jar format).

New Jersey ($6.51/gram) shows rapid price compression (−21.8% year-over-year) in a maturing East Coast market, declining from $8.33 in June 2025 as retail competition intensifies across the state's expanding dispensary network. The figure is the adult-use Bud/Flower price per gram for June 2026, the most recent month reported in the Cannabis Regulatory Commission's board meeting data.

Ohio ($6.55/gram) shows elevated pricing in a young adult-use market (sales began August 2024) constrained by limited licensing and high demand from a large population base; prices have begun to compress as expanded cultivation capacity comes online.

Connecticut ($6.57/gram) shows pricing typical of a maturing Northeast market that launched adult-use sales in January 2023, where prices remain elevated as retail infrastructure continues to develop.

West Virginia ($7.14/gram) is a medical-only program whose pricing has followed a sustained downward trajectory — down approximately 15.4% from $8.43/gram in October 2023, with a brief peak of $8.03/gram in July 2024 before resuming its decline. The compression is supply-driven: total harvested weight grew from roughly 10,100 lbs/month at the start of the reporting period to a peak of 25,600 lbs in October 2024, nearly tripling production capacity. With no adult-use market creating competitive pressure, WV's price decline reflects maturation of its licensed grower and processor base rather than structural market competition. Data sourced from the WV Office of Medical Cannabis 2025 Biennial Report (Metrc seed-to-sale).

Vermont ($7.37/gram)‡ is one of the few markets where prices rose after launch: July 2023 flooding hit its outdoor-heavy supply, and weighted average prices climbed from $7.73 at market open to $11.37 by Q2 2024, per a February 2026 CPPC report. Prices have since eased as output recovered, reaching $8.91 by September 2025 in the Cannabis Control Board's monthly data. The current figure is the CCB's November 2025 flower average; an October 2025 point-of-sale weight correction means earlier monthly figures may be overstated by up to 15%, so some of the drop to $7.37 reflects reporting rather than the market.

Pennsylvania ($7.49/gram) maintains higher pricing typical of a medical-only program with restricted licensing and no adult-use market to drive competitive pressure.

Maryland ($7.68/gram)¶ represents elevated pricing characteristic of a newer market (adult-use sales began July 2023) still developing cultivation capacity and distribution networks.

High-price markets (over $8/gram)

Arkansas ($8.10/gram) is a medical-only program whose figure represents a blended average across all product types — derived from official DFA annual totals ($291.1M total revenue ÷ 79,223 lbs sold). Arkansas does not publish category-level data, so no flower-only price is available; the true flower price likely sits below this figure, as concentrates and edibles carry higher per-gram-equivalent values in a blended average.

Hawaii ($9.20/gram) reflects pricing in a medical-only island market with just 25 dispensaries serving 1.45 million residents; geographic isolation drives inherent cost premiums.

Washington DC ($10.03/gram) reflects pricing in a medical-only program with no adult-use retail competition; the figure is the regular Flower/Bud category from the ABCA Medical Cannabis Program Report (September 2026), calculated as retail flower sales divided by weight sold, and excludes pre-rolls, shake/trim, and concentrates.

Virginia ($10.31/gram)¶ shows pricing typical of a market in its earliest stages, with limited retail infrastructure driving higher costs.

New York ($10.61/gram)† reflects the challenges of a large-state rollout marked by significant licensing delays and a deeply entrenched illicit market that has suppressed legal retail expansion; despite New York's large population base and long-term competitive potential, the 2024 annual average from the OCM Market Report places it among the nation's most expensive legal markets.

Alaska ($10.94/gram)‖ reflects pricing in a small, geographically isolated adult-use market where, in 2022, cultivators paid a weight-based excise tax of $50 per ounce of mature bud ($25 for immature bud, $15 for trim) — a fixed cost of roughly $1.76 per gram that does not shrink as retail prices fall. The figure is Bud/Flower retail sales divided by weight sold for calendar year 2022 ($91.68M ÷ 18,484 lbs) from the Alaska Marijuana Control Board's Retail Sales by Product Type report, published January 2023; it excludes the separately reported Immature Bud category ($10.10/gram). Although this is a historical figure, price compression in Alaska has been tepid since, and it remains a reasonable benchmark for current pricing.

Minnesota ($14.23/gram)§ shows the highest prices in the nation, reflecting an extremely new market (adult-use sales began August 2024) with very limited retail infrastructure and cultivator capacity. As these markets mature, prices are expected to decline toward the national median.

Data sources and methodology

All pricing data is sourced directly from official state cannabis regulatory agencies. We compile the most recent available data for each state, which typically represents average or median retail pricing for cannabis flower (also called "buds" or "usable marijuana"). Data collection dates vary by state based on their reporting schedules - most states publish monthly or quarterly market data.

Crucially, these figures reflect realized retail prices — what consumers actually paid — rather than advertised menu prices. Where states report total retail revenue and volume sold, the per-gram figure is calculated as revenue divided by quantity (for example, Oklahoma's $19.46M ÷ 8.04M grams), which inherently captures discounts, promotions, and loyalty pricing because those reductions are already embedded in the recorded sales totals. This is a fundamental methodological distinction from menu-scraping price tools, which aggregate undiscounted list prices and therefore systematically overstate what people actually spend. A minority of states are sourced from official price studies and regulatory market analyses (noted individually); these report measured or weighted-average transaction prices rather than scraped menus, and the methodology for each is disclosed in the per-state notes.

Price calculations are standardized to per-gram pricing for direct comparison across markets. Where a state reports a price per eighth-ounce package, the per-gram figure divides by the 3.5 g net weight printed on the package (as with California, Maine, and Mississippi); where it reports a price per ounce, it divides by 28.35 g (as with Michigan). Some states report wholesale prices, while others report retail prices inclusive of state taxes. Where applicable, notes indicate the specific calculation methodology used. All source links connect directly to official state data portals for transparency and verification.

Cannabis price trends and market outlook

The overall trend in cannabis pricing shows continued decline as markets mature. Colorado, for example, has seen prices drop from over $9/gram in early years to current levels around $3/gram. This pattern reflects increasing cultivation efficiency, market competition, and reduced regulatory uncertainty. Newer markets typically start with higher prices that gradually decrease as supply chains optimize and more licenses are issued.

Federal prohibition remains the primary factor maintaining price disparities across state markets. Interstate commerce restrictions prevent efficient arbitrage between low-cost and high-cost markets. If federal legalization occurs, experts predict significant price convergence, with high-cost markets likely to see the most dramatic price reductions as cultivators in low-cost production regions gain access to national distribution.
